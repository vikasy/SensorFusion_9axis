1 Introduction 
1.1 Summary 
Quaternions are hyper-complex numbers of rank 4 tha t were invented by William Rowan Hamilton in 
1843.    They are commonly used to simplify computa tion of 3-dimensional rotations. 
This application note contains an introduction to q uaternion algebra and its use to represent rotation s. It 
documents the specific quaternion functions used in  the Freescale Sensor Fusion Library  within the file 
orientation.c . 
The four components of a quaternion provide a more efficient means, both in storage and computation, 
to represent orientations as compared to the nine c omponents of a rotation matrix. Quaternions are 
therefore used extensively in computer gaming and h ave, in consequence, also been adopted as a 
standard for representing orientation in sensor fus ion applications. 
Rotation matrices and rotation quaternions share th e very important characteristic of being well 
behaved mathematically compared to the Euler angles  (roll, pitch and yaw). The phenomenon of gimbal 
lock instability in strapdown sensor systems occurs  in the Euler angle representation of orientation, 
typically at ±90 o pitch, but is entirely absent in rotation matrix o r rotation quaternion representations 
which are stable and continuous. Euler angles are t herefore only used in the Freescale Sensor Fusion 
Library  as an output representation of calculation perform ed using rotation matrices and rotation 
quaternions.
